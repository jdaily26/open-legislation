# Model State Bill

Version 1.1

## Data Center Community Benefits Act

An Act relating to public financial assistance for data centers, establishing enforceable community benefits agreements, and providing for oversight, public reporting, and enforcement.

Be it enacted by the Legislature of this State:

## Sec. 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Data Center Community Benefits Act.

## Sec. 2. Definitions

(a) “Data center” means a facility, campus, or part of either primarily used to house computing equipment for data processing, storage, or transmission, including related power, cooling, and other infrastructure serving that development.

(b) “Local government” means a municipality or county, including a local authority or special-purpose entity acting on its behalf.

(c) “Covered incentive” means public financial assistance for construction, expansion, or operation of a data center that a local government provides, approves, arranges, or administers through a discretionary decision, regardless of its form. Assistance includes grants, loans and loan guarantees, tax abatements or credits, negotiated rebates, payments in lieu of taxes, tax increment financing, land or leases below appraised market value, waived fees, below-market utility service, infrastructure cost-sharing, and in-kind goods or services. Assistance that depends on a local approval is covered even when another public body supplies it.

(d) “Generally available provision” means a provision enacted before the first application, with identical terms for all similarly situated applicants and automatic eligibility under objective criteria. A provision enacted in the preceding 12 months that in practice fits only the applicant or its affiliates does not qualify. Ordinary permitting and assistance granted solely under a generally available provision are excluded. Other costs associated with the same project shall be disclosed under subsection (g).

(e) “Project” means all phases, facilities, and affiliated entities in a common development plan, and any portions receiving assistance within a 60-month period that share control, occupy contiguous or functionally related parcels, or are represented as one development. Mere proximity alone does not combine independently operated, unrelated developments.

(f) “Recipient” means the entity receiving a covered incentive. “Responsible party” means a recipient, ultimate controlling parent, named site operator, or successor bound under section 4.

(g) “Total estimated public cost” means the estimated public value committed to or caused by a covered project over the full term of the award, using a consistent published valuation method and excluding duplicate costs. It includes direct expenditures, foregone revenue, below-market property or services, any state assistance triggered by the award, administrative and monitoring costs, and unreimbursed costs for roads, grid interconnection or upgrades, water supply or treatment, and other infrastructure borne by public entities or other ratepayers. The estimate shall show sources, material assumptions, the cost bearer, and reasonably foreseeable costs that cannot yet be priced. The local government shall use amounts allocated under applicable tariffs and orders for regulated utility costs and account for enforceable applicant reimbursements. It shall document the expected timing and valuation of each component.

## Sec. 3. Application and aggregation

(a) Duties under this Act begin at the earlier of the filing of a request for a covered incentive or the first project-specific commitment of public funds, property, or services toward the proposed incentive. Notice, independent review, and the agreement required by section 4 shall precede any binding award, transfer of value, or disbursement. A local government shall apply this Act to a proposed award, renewal, or material amendment.

(b) The local government shall aggregate covered incentives, phases, and related applications for the same project. Applicants shall disclose other pending or awarded assistance for the project. Participating local governments may designate a joint review lead but remain responsible for their own awards. An arrangement with the predominant purpose of avoiding this Act shall be treated as covered. The applicant bears the burden of showing eligibility for an exclusion or rebutting a finding of avoidance on stated facts.

(c) A material amendment includes a change to the project scope, term, responsible parties, beneficiary, benefit value or delivery, verification, disclosure, security, or remedies. A cumulative increase of 10 percent or more in covered assistance or estimated public cost, measured against the last approval following public notice, is material. When an updated estimate reaches that threshold, the local government shall pause further disbursements and apply section 4 before approving or paying the balance. Renewals trigger review regardless of a change in amount.

## Sec. 4. Public review and required agreement

(a) Before formally accepting applications, the local government shall publish a needs assessment identifying affected areas, local priorities, and the facts supporting them. An outside assessor shall be selected by competitive procurement and certify independence in writing. The applicant and its affiliates may provide evidence through the public process. They may neither select the assessor nor direct its work. When an unsolicited request starts review under section 3, the local government shall complete this assessment before it formally considers the request.

(b) At least 45 calendar days before voting, the local government shall post at one public address the complete proposed agreement, the cost estimate, the needs assessment, and a plain-language summary of no more than two pages. The summary shall state the type, amount, and term of assistance, the benefit value and percentage of public cost, each metric and verification method, the security amount and form, the parent, operator, and guarantor, and the terms for transfer. The local government shall accept written comments, hold an accessible public hearing, and post a table responding to each written comment and each distinct hearing issue at least seven days before the vote. Identical comments may be grouped if the table accounts for every submission. Any change to proposed terms other than a clerical correction, or a cumulative increase of at least 10 percent in the noticed cost, requires renewed notice and hearing. Proposed material amendments after approval follow the same procedure.

(c) Before voting, the local government shall post expected benefits and recipients by district or affected area, governing-body disclosures of reportable campaign contributions from the applicant, affiliates, and project contractors over the preceding 24 months, and any lobbying, consulting, or advisory engagement of a former local official by those parties within 24 months of leaving office. The applicant shall certify its disclosures after reasonable inquiry. A decision maker with a disqualifying interest shall recuse under applicable ethics law.

(d) Before approval, the local government shall execute a written agreement, contingent on approval, with the recipient, ultimate controlling parent if any, and each entity that will operate the facility. The parties are jointly and severally liable. The agreement shall set out each benefit’s recipient, measurable baseline and service, responsible party, funding, payment and delivery dates, independent verification, and remedies. A parent guarantee or additional independent credit support shall be required if audited financial statements show that the responsible parties lack adequate capacity to cover outstanding obligations. The local government shall publish its credit findings.

(e) The aggregate verified value committed to community benefits shall be at least 15 percent of total estimated public cost. For purposes of this Act, required benefit value is the full value of benefits the agreement commits to deliver. At least 40 percent of that value shall be cash or cash equivalent payable to a public entity or an independent nonprofit without common control, board control, or a material financial conflict with a responsible party. Choose benefits in at least three categories from workforce, water, energy and grid, housing, childcare and transit, education, community funds, and infrastructure. At least one category shall require a cash expenditure verified by a party independent of the applicant and beneficiary. A duty already required by law, permit, tariff, or other binding obligation earns no benefit credit. In-kind value shall be the lower of documented cost or independently determined fair market value. If an approved cost revision raises the floor, the agreement shall fund the difference before further disbursements.

(f) The local government shall match installments or other transfers of public value to verified delivery. Cumulative disbursement shall not exceed the share of authorized assistance corresponding to the share of required benefit value independently verified as delivered. Before delivery reaches 50 percent of required value, cumulative disbursement shall not exceed 25 percent of authorized covered assistance. Advance deposits for later services fund performance but count toward the delivery threshold only when the underlying work is verified. The agreement shall specify each holdback, disbursement condition, repayment amount or formula, and evidence needed for release.

(g) Before the award becomes effective and until all duties are verified, the responsible parties shall maintain security at least equal to the independently valued cost of all remaining benefits. The security shall be an irrevocable standby letter of credit or a surety bond from an independent issuer rated A or equivalent or higher, payable on documented demand by an independent trustee designated by the local government. The instrument shall cover at least 120 days after the final scheduled duty and require advance notice of nonrenewal. The agreement shall require replenishment within 15 calendar days after a draw and replacement before any lapse or loss of eligibility. The trustee shall safeguard the proceeds for performance or recovery under section 7. The recipient shall fund set-up and operating costs in advance of each service period through the agreement’s dedicated account.

(h) While duties remain outstanding, a responsible party shall obtain prior written consent for a transfer of the facility or award, a cumulative direct or indirect change of 25 percent or more in equity or voting interests, a merger or consolidation, a ground lease of five years or more, a change of operator, a foreclosure transfer, or a sale-leaseback. Consent requires written assumption, a successor or guarantor with credit at least equal to that of the party being replaced, and continued security without interruption. Credit findings and the assumption shall be posted. A successor that takes the facility or award is bound by outstanding duties regardless of private assumption language, subject to controlling law. The predecessor and guarantors remain liable until the local government releases them in writing after confirming replacement credit and security. A responsible party shall provide for continuity of records, reporting, and performance before dissolution. Duties are complete only after the independent monitor verifies them in writing.

(i) The agreement shall set limits on distributions, affiliate management fees, and affiliate debt secured by the facility while duties remain outstanding, or require independently secured additional coverage for the resulting exposure. The local government shall publish its basis for the limits or added coverage and independently review related-party transactions before releasing assistance.

(j) Duties shall be stated as measurable outcomes. A best-efforts obligation may apply only to a supplemental duty, using the efforts a prudent operator would use to meet a binding financing covenant. An event outside a responsible party’s reasonable control may extend a nonpayment duty only upon written notice within 10 days after discovery and contemporaneous evidence of its effect and mitigation. Any such extension is capped at 12 months in total and shall not excuse payment or maintenance of security. Market prices, financing costs, and ordinary business conditions are excluded. An amendment shall be written and posted. A contract term that waives a requirement of this Act is unenforceable.

(k) The agreement shall include a decommissioning and site-restoration plan with separately sized financial assurance, a water withdrawal and consumption cap based on local supply with a reclaimed-water requirement where independently found feasible, independent metering, limits and logs for generator operating and testing hours with defined emergency exceptions, and a measurable property-line noise limit assessed by independent baseline and operational testing. The applicant shall fund its lawfully allocable share of project-specific grid interconnection or upgrades under applicable tariffs and orders. The agreement shall state who pays, how each limit is measured, who verifies it, and what happens if it is missed. Other permitting obligations remain in effect. Compliance with an independently required baseline shall not count toward the benefit floor.

## Sec. 5. Records, verification, and public notice

(a) Within 30 calendar days of signing, the local government shall post the agreement and later amendments. The public record shall include the agreement, benefit metrics, verification findings, credit support amount, issuer, expiration, and draw conditions. Withholding requires a specific basis in controlling law. Other records are presumed public unless a specific legal exemption applies. The local government shall post an itemized log identifying the basis for each withholding. Personal identifiers and legally protected records shall be handled under applicable law, with public aggregates sufficient to verify stated benefit metrics.

(b) The recipient shall preserve records for 10 years after the agreement ends and deliver records within 30 calendar days of a written request. It shall report before each incentive release, within 60 days of each award anniversary, and within 60 days after the agreement ends. Reports shall use a prescribed machine-readable form showing payments due and paid, actual service against a baseline, independent source records, successors, outstanding obligations, and security remaining. A responsible corporate officer shall certify material statements under penalty of perjury.

(c) A materially false or misleading certification is a breach. For a knowing material misstatement of benefit value, the local government shall seek a civil recovery of twice the misstated value, subject to section 7. The government shall verify reported figures against independent evidence before crediting delivery. The recipient bears the burden of producing the required records.

## Sec. 6. Independent monitoring

(a) The governing body shall appoint a full-time public employee as independent monitor. A local government may share a qualified public employee through an interlocal agreement. The monitor reports directly to the governing body and shall have no project recruitment, negotiation, or award approval duties and no financial or employment relationship with a responsible party. Before voting, the governing body shall appropriate funds for monitoring and annual independent audit through a dedicated public line item equal to at least 1 percent of total estimated public cost over the full term. It shall publish the appropriation, scope, and funding source. Any reimbursement required from the recipient shall use public accounts under local control.

(b) The recipient shall provide access to the facility, meters, supporting books, payroll, and provider records as needed for verification on 10 calendar days’ written notice, subject to applicable access and privacy rules. The monitor shall verify each release and commission an annual independent audit. The government shall furnish a public channel for submitting evidence of a breach. Within 45 calendar days of receiving credible evidence, the monitor shall issue a breach notice or a written, reviewable determination stating why one is unwarranted.

## Sec. 7. Enforcement and review

(a) On documented evidence of breach, the monitor shall give written notice of the facts, the duty, the correction required, and the review procedure. The local government shall immediately hold unpaid assistance while the breach is pending. A responsible party has 30 calendar days after receipt to cure a first breach of a duty and 15 days to request review. A repeat breach of the same duty during the agreement term carries no new cure period. Failure to replenish or replace security shall be addressed by the earliest applicable deadline under section 4(g), including any earlier deadline necessary to prevent lapse.

(b) An independent hearing officer who did not issue the notice, negotiate the terms, or approve the award shall allow the parties to respond and issue a reasoned written decision within 30 days of a request. Review does not restore disbursements pending decision. The hearing officer may grant interim relief for good cause. A court may review the order under applicable law.

(c) On a confirmed and uncured breach, the local government shall withhold further disbursement, require repayment of assistance attributable to unperformed or falsely certified benefits, and direct the independent trustee to draw the amount needed for overdue performance, replacement service, or recovery as specified in the instrument. On an uncured failure to renew, replace, or replenish security, the trustee shall draw available coverage for outstanding obligations and hold proceeds for performance or lawful recovery. The agreement shall permit step-in or substitute performance at the responsible parties’ reasonable expense. The agreement shall set daily liquidated damages where loss is difficult to measure and the amount reasonably forecasts that loss. Overdue monetary amounts bear interest at the applicable judgment rate. Remedies shall avoid double recovery. Enforcement and acceleration of due obligations are subject to federal bankruptcy law and any lawful stay.

(d) A resident of an affected area, an organization representing affected residents, or a participating local government may bring a civil action in a court of competent jurisdiction to enforce this Act or the agreement or to compel a required act. A claimant other than a participating government shall give written notice to the monitor and responsible parties and may bring suit after 45 days if the matter remains unresolved. A court may hear an earlier request for urgent relief to prevent loss of security or immediate harm. A covered resident or organization need not show special injury or post a bond solely to bring an action under this subsection, subject to controlling jurisdictional and court rules. A prevailing resident or organization shall recover reasonable attorney fees and costs. A prevailing local government may recover reasonable enforcement costs as provided in the agreement.

## Sec. 8. Reporting and evaluation

(a) Within 30 days after each report is due, the local government shall post the monitor’s independent findings and annual audit in a public register. Include the project-wide public cost and amounts paid, obligations and verified benefits by district or affected area, security coverage, missed deadlines, complaints and decisions, remedies, and administrative costs. Identify unverified benefits as undelivered for purposes of disbursement. Post a notice of any missed reporting or enforcement deadline and the next required action.

(b) The local government shall record outcomes at baseline, annually, and at closeout. It shall retain the findings with the agreement and publish a final accounting of funds and service delivery. A successor approval, lawful release of security, or determination of completed performance shall be documented in the register.

## Sec. 9. Transition, severability, and effective date

(a) This Act takes effect on the 180th day after enactment. It applies to awards, renewals, material amendments, and commitments approved on or after that date. A project with a prior award that has not begun substantial construction by the effective date shall comply before a subsequent discretionary approval or disbursement to the extent permitted by existing contractual rights and controlling law. A later renewal or material amendment is subject to the Act even when the original award predates it.

(b) If a court holds any provision or application invalid, the remaining provisions and applications that can operate independently shall remain in effect.
