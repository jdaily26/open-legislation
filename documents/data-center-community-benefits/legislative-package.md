## Sponsor Cover Note

Re: Enforceable community benefits for data-center incentives

Dear Legislative Director,

When a city or county considers a grant or negotiated tax rebate for a data center, residents should be able to see the price and the benefits before the vote. The bill requires public review, a signed agreement, and proof of delivery. Each community can choose the benefit that fits its needs.

Consider a library that could use the computers a developer is offering. In the worked example, a signed agreement would also fund 12 extra staffed computer-lab hours a week for three years. Residents could see whether the developer paid on time and whether the library delivered those hours.

Would your office ask legislative counsel to review the bill and discuss it with city finance staff, public library operators, and residents before a committee hearing?

## Model State Bill

Version 1.0

### Data Center Community Benefits Act

An Act relating to local financial incentives for data centers and requiring enforceable community benefits agreements before certain awards.

Be it enacted by the Legislature of this State:

### Sec. 1. Short title

This Act may be cited as the Data Center Community Benefits Act.

### Sec. 2. Definitions

(a) “Data center” means a facility, or a portion of a facility, primarily used to house computing equipment for data processing, storage, or transmission.

(b) “Local government” means a municipality or county.

(c) “Covered incentive” means a discretionary cash grant or a negotiated rebate of local taxes that a local government awards for construction or expansion of a data center. A generally available tax provision is excluded.

(d) “Recipient” means the entity receiving a covered incentive and any successor that assumes its duties under section 4.

### Sec. 3. Application

This Act applies when a local government proposes to award, renew, or materially increase a covered incentive. The incentive and any phased payments for the same project shall be reviewed together. Other legal requirements for the project remain in effect.

### Sec. 4. Required action

(a) At least 30 calendar days before voting, the local government shall publish the proposed agreement, total estimated public cost, and a plain-language summary. It shall accept written comments and hold a public hearing before voting. Before the vote, it shall publish a response to material issues raised.

(b) Before approving a covered incentive, the local government shall execute a written agreement with the applicant that takes effect upon approval. It shall state each benefit, the intended beneficiaries, responsible parties, the amount or measurable service, start and end dates, payment and delivery schedules, proof required, and remedies. The local government shall select benefits based on documented community needs and explain its selection in its public response.

(c) The agreement shall identify the funding source for set-up and ongoing costs and require financial security for unpaid obligations that extend past the final scheduled incentive payment. It shall require prior written consent, written assumption of duties, and continued security before any transfer of the facility or award while duties remain outstanding.

(d) If proposed terms materially reduce a benefit or increase public cost after publication, the local government shall repeat the notice and hearing under subsection (a) before voting.

### Sec. 5. Records and notice

(a) The local government shall post the signed agreement and amendments within 30 calendar days of execution. It shall protect information as required by applicable public records law.

(b) The recipient shall keep records of amounts due and paid and evidence of delivery for three years after the agreement ends. It shall provide requested records within 30 calendar days of a written request and submit a performance report within 60 calendar days of each award anniversary until the agreement ends. A final report is due within 60 calendar days after the agreement ends.

### Sec. 6. Administration

The governing body shall designate an officer to review submissions, verify performance, and enforce the agreement. Before the vote, it shall identify how monitoring will be staffed and funded.

### Sec. 7. Enforcement and review

(a) The designated officer may examine evidence of compliance. If the officer finds a breach, the officer shall give the recipient written notice stating the facts, the duty breached, the correction required, and how to request review.

(b) The recipient has 30 calendar days after receipt to correct the breach or request review. The governing body, through a decision maker who did not issue the notice, shall provide an opportunity to respond and issue a written decision within 30 calendar days of the request.

(c) If a breach remains uncorrected after the correction period and any timely review, the local government shall withhold unpaid installments until correction and may seek recovery or draw on security as the agreement permits. Judicial review remains available under applicable law.

### Sec. 8. Reporting and evaluation

Within 30 calendar days after each report is due, the designated officer shall publish findings for the governing body. The findings shall show the incentive awarded and paid, amounts owed and paid by the recipient, services promised and verified against the agreement, reported gaps, corrective actions, and local monitoring costs.

### Sec. 9. Transition and effective date

This Act takes effect on the 180th day after enactment and applies to covered incentives first awarded, renewed, or materially increased on or after that date.

## Additional Model Clauses

Three optional terms a city could add to its agreement. The library schedule shows how a city could turn the terms into a service people can use.

### Advance Payment and Security

The recipient shall pay the equipment contribution 30 calendar days before services begin and each quarterly operating contribution before the quarter begins. Before the city pays the rebate, the recipient shall provide a letter of credit covering the unpaid benefit commitments. The city may draw on it for an uncured payment default under the agreement and section 7 of the Act.

### Transfer

The recipient shall provide at least 60 calendar days’ written notice of a proposed transfer of the facility or award while duties remain outstanding. As conditions of written consent, the city shall require the successor’s written assumption of the remaining obligations and confirmation that equivalent security will be in force at transfer.

### Unavailable or Delayed Service

The library shall record each missed session and offer replacement hours within 30 calendar days. If the library cannot continue the service, the city shall publish a replacement plan and seek public comment before agreeing to a material change. The agreement shall keep unused funds restricted to the approved benefit or its approved replacement.

### Worked Agreement Schedule

| **Term** | **Example commitment** |
| --- | --- |
| Incentive | Up to $1,000,000 in one negotiated local tax rebate. Payment follows the first month of service and proof of advance funding and security. |
| Local benefit | Use the existing branch library. Add 12 staffed computer-lab hours each week for three years, including one evening each week. Begin within 30 days after the data center opens. |
| Developer payment | $10,000 for equipment and accessibility, plus $30,000 a year for operations. Prepay $7,500 per quarter. Total committed over three years is $100,000. |
| Initial security | $82,500 when the rebate is paid, assuming the set-up amount and first quarter have been prepaid. Keep coverage in force for the remaining balance as advances are made. |
| Proof and duties | Developer provides payment receipts. Library runs the lab and records hours and attendance. City finance verifies funding and service, then posts results. |

## Committee Fact Sheet

Data Center Community Benefits Act  |  Version 1.0

### Summary

Before a city or county gives a data center a discretionary cash grant or negotiated local tax rebate, it would have to show the public cost, hear residents, and secure an enforceable benefits agreement. The committee is asked to hear the bill.

### Background

Virginia’s legislative audit agency found that most data-center job gains were in construction. Lancaster’s published agreement summary describes scheduled payments and financial security. New Jersey’s economic development agency provides resources to help towns assess proposed projects. \[1–3\]

### The Problem

A pledge to buy computers can leave a city with equipment and no budget for evening staff. The city needs the cost of operating the service, a payment schedule, and a way for residents to see whether the service happened.

### The Solution

Post the terms 30 days before the vote. Hear residents and respond to material issues. Sign a deal with deadlines, proof, and remedies. Publish what the developer paid and what the community received. In the worked example, the developer commits $100,000 to equip and staff an existing library lab for three years. (Secs. 4–8)

### Support

Invite residents, library staff, and city finance teams to review the draft, price the work, and state their position before the hearing.

## Bill Analysis and Evidence

The bill sets a process for discretionary local incentives. The city or county chooses the benefit and weighs it alongside the full public cost of the deal.

### Current Law and Authority

For each state, identify the statutes that authorize local grants and tax rebates, who votes, and how notice, hearings, and public records work. Use the state’s existing authority for incentive awards and add the steps this bill requires before an award.

### Section Summary

| **Bill section** | **What changes** | **Who acts and when** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secs. 1–3 | Defines the local awards that trigger the law. | City or county checks coverage at the start. |
| Sec. 4 | Requires notice, hearing, responses, and a signed agreement. | Local government starts public review at least 30 days before a vote. |
| Secs. 5–6 | Requires records and designates an officer to check them. | Recipient reports annually. Local government names an officer and funds monitoring before a vote. |
| Secs. 7–8 | Requires a correction opportunity and makes results public. | Officer gives notice if there is a breach. Review body acts within 30 days of a request. |
| Sec. 9 | Sets the effective date and the first covered awards. | Applies 180 days after enactment. |

### Evidence

| **Source** | **Finding** | **Use in the bill** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Virginia JLARC \[1\] | Employment gains were mainly in construction. | Show the timing of promised benefits and verify them after opening. |

## Fiscal Worksheet

Version 1.0  |  One city, one award, three operating years  |  Worked example

**Cost basis** The city already has a finance office and a branch library. The developer funds new equipment and lab operations. The city funds review and verification.

| **Who pays** | **Set-up** | **Each year** | **Three-year total** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City monitoring | $3,000 | $4,500 | $16,500 |
| Developer benefit | $10,000 | $30,000 | $100,000 |
| City rebate | Up to $1,000,000 paid once after service starts | Single payment | Up to $1,000,000 |

### One-Time and Recurring Work

The city budgets 40 staff hours to set up the process and 60 hours per year to check records and report results, each at $75 per hour including benefits and overhead. The library budgets $10,000 for equipment and accessibility. Its annual operating budget is $24,960 for staffing (12 × 52 × $40), plus $5,040 for connectivity and maintenance.

### Funding and Revenue

The city general fund pays $16,500 for monitoring. The proposed rebate would reduce city revenue by up to $1 million in the payment year. The developer prepays $7,500 each quarter to a dedicated library account. The bill assigns no recurring state administrative duties.

### Uncertainty

If verification takes 40–80 hours per year, monitoring would cost $12,000–$21,000 over three years including set-up. Actual costs depend on payroll, equipment needs, dispute volume, and the number of projects.

**Fiscal review** Ask the city finance director to verify rebate authority, timing, and foregone revenue. Ask the library director to price staffing, accessibility, and service continuity.

## Implementation and Evaluation

The developer funds the service, the library runs it, and the city verifies what happened.

### Implementation Schedule

| **Milestone** | **Owner** | **Due** | **Proof** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publish proposal | City clerk | 30 days before vote | Draft agreement, cost, summary, hearing notice |
| Set benefit terms | City and developer | Before vote | Public responses and signed agreement contingent on approval |
| Prepare service | Library and developer | Before first session | Equipment, accessible workstations, funds, and schedule |
| Open service | Library | Within 30 days of data center opening | Posted hours and first session log |
| Report delivery | Developer / city finance | 60 days after each award anniversary / 30 days after report due | Report and published verification |

### Test a Real Case

If a quarterly payment is late, city finance checks the deposit records and sends written notice. The agreement gives the developer 30 days to pay or request review under section 7. After the correction period and any timely review, the city can draw on the letter of credit for an unpaid amount the agreement covers.

### Records and Privacy

City finance checks payment receipts and the library’s session logs. Publish aggregate attendance and service hours. Keep individual patron records out of public reports.

### Evaluation Plan

| **Measure** | **Starting point** | **Target** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Additional staffed hours | Record the branch’s existing schedule before launch. | 624 additional hours per year, including weekly evening availability. |
| Operating payments on time | First advance starts the record. | All 12 quarterly advances received before each quarter. |
| Use of evening sessions | Record attendance in the first quarter. | Report quarterly visits and use the results to adjust schedules. |

**Decision after review** At the end of year one, the city checks paid amounts, hours, and attendance. Address any shortfall under the agreement and assess whether a schedule change would help residents use the lab.

## Hearing and Testimony Kit

Ask the committee to advance Version 1.0 after hearing from residents and local staff.

### Sponsor Opening

Chair and members of the committee, consider a data-center developer offering computers to a city library. The computers are useful when people can get to them and the library can staff the lab. This bill makes the city show residents the deal before voting and write down who pays, when service starts, and how delivery will be checked. I ask the committee to advance the bill.

### Sample Library Testimony

To add 12 staffed hours a week, including an evening, our branch would need a schedule and a recurring budget. At $40 per hour with benefits and overhead, staffing alone would cost $24,960 a year. Advance funding lets a branch schedule staff before promising hours. Published logs let residents see whether those hours happened.

### Witness Plan

| **Perspective** | **What they can establish** | **Next step** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Library patron or resident | Which hours and accessibility features would help. | Invite to share firsthand experience. |
| Library director | Staffing cost, delivery, and closures. | Request review of the service budget. |
| City finance officer | Valuation of rebate and verification of payments. | Request fiscal and enforcement review. |
| Developer representative | Timing of funding, cost of security, and feasibility. | Request response to proposed terms. |

### Expected Questions and Responses

Will this delay projects? Section 4 sets a 30-day notice period. A city can schedule it alongside its existing reviews. A new round is required if the deal gets materially worse for the public.  
Who decides whether the rebate is worth it? The governing body does. The notice lists public costs so residents can challenge the case before the vote.  
What if the developer sells? Section 4 requires written assumption of the duties and continued security before transfer of the facility or award while duties remain outstanding.

**Coordination** Prepare a two-minute opening and submit written testimony by the committee’s posted deadline.
