# Rebuttals

Data Center Community Benefits Act · Bill v1.1

## Cost and scope

- **Will this make it harder to attract a data center?** The bill adds obligations to public deals and gives developers the terms upfront so they can price them into the project.
- **Why require 15%?** The bill sets a minimum based on the full estimated public cost and lets communities help choose what the money supports.
- **What if residents still pay for grid or water upgrades?** The public estimate includes unreimbursed grid and water costs, and the developer pays its assigned share of project-specific grid work under applicable utility rates and orders.
- **Does every data center need an agreement?** The requirement applies when a local government provides, approves, arranges, or administers discretionary public assistance for a project.

## Delivery and enforcement

- **Can a developer count work it already has to do?** Only benefits beyond duties already required by law, permit, or another binding obligation count toward the minimum.
- **What stops a big payout on a promise?** At most 25% of the assistance can go out before an independent monitor verifies half the promised benefit value.
- **Can the developer sell the site and walk away?** A covered transfer requires local consent, a successor that takes on the remaining duties, and a letter of credit or bond that stays in place.
- **What if the city ignores a breach?** Affected residents and community groups can give notice and seek enforcement in court after 45 days if the issue remains unresolved.
- **Will residents see the deal in time to weigh in?** The city must share the proposed terms at least 45 days before voting and post its responses at least seven days before the vote.
