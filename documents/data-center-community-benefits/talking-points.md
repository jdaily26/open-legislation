# Talking points

Data Center Community Benefits Act · Bill v1.1

## Opening line

“If a city helps pay for a data center, residents should see the full cost, have a say in the deal, and be able to check what they got.”

## What to emphasize

- **Show the full price before the vote.** Count the public cost of the whole project, including unreimbursed grid and water costs, and give residents 45 days to review the deal. *(Sections 2–4)*
- **Fund something neighbors can use.** At least 15% of estimated public cost goes to benefits such as staffed library access, job training, or help paying for transit. *(Section 4)*
- **Make payments follow delivery.** An independent monitor checks what residents received before the city releases assistance. *(Sections 4 and 6)*
- **Keep the promises through a sale.** Covered transfers require a successor to take on the remaining duties and keep the letter of credit or bond in place. *(Section 4)*
- **Give residents a way to act.** The city posts results, and affected residents can seek enforcement if a breach remains unresolved after notice. *(Sections 7–8)*

## Example story

“Imagine you finish work just as your library closes. A data center asks the city for help, and the city shares its $1.2 million public cost estimate before anyone votes. Under the bill, that deal would need at least $180,000 in benefits. In this example, $100,000 equips the library and funds 12 extra staffed computer-lab hours a week for three years, including one evening. The rest goes to job training and transit fares. The city checks the hours people actually got before paying out assistance. If a missed payment goes unresolved, the trustee can draw on a letter of credit to keep services running.”

## Close with an ask

“If your city is weighing a data-center incentive, ask to see the full cost, what people will get, and who will check that it happened.”
