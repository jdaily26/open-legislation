# Data Center Community Benefits Act

Give residents a say in what a city secures before it commits public money to a data center.

## Summary

Ask a state sponsor to circulate a bill requiring a public benefits agreement before a city or county awards a discretionary cash grant or tax rebate to a new or expanding data center. Residents could inspect the deal before the vote and track delivery afterward.

## Background

Construction and operations bring different benefits. In Virginia’s legislative review, industry representatives estimated about 50 ongoing jobs at a typical 250,000-square-foot facility, versus around 1,500 workers on site at peak construction. These estimates describe different phases of a project. [1]

## The problem

Some neighbors in Virginia reported that persistent data-center noise affected their well-being. [1] A community benefit should accompany enforceable mitigation. Suppose a developer offers to equip a neighborhood center. The public presentation includes computers, but the offer leaves staffing and opening hours unresolved. Residents have no way to tell whether the center will be open when they need it. The council still has to decide whether to award the rebate.

## The solution

Publish proposed terms at least 30 days before the vote and hear residents before approval. For each benefit, name the payer, recipient, amount or service, due date and proof. Require annual public accounts and suspend unpaid incentives if a material breach goes uncured. The locality would budget for review and monitoring before committing funds.
