# Data Center Community Benefits

Policy brief | Version 1.1

## Decision

Choose a state and ask a legislative office to review a bill that conditions discretionary city and county assistance to data centers on measurable benefits for residents.

## Problem

A promise to buy library computers leaves open who pays to keep the lab staffed. Before approving a grant, rebate, land discount, or other negotiated assistance, a local government should know what the project costs the public, what residents will get, and who will still owe it if the facility changes hands.

## Recommendation

Review connected phases together. Count the costs that fall on public entities and other utility customers. Give residents 45 days’ notice and publish responses at least seven days before the vote. Require benefits worth at least 15% of public cost, with at least 40% of benefit value in cash or cash equivalent and services in at least three categories.

Let residents identify priorities through the hearing. Require the developer, controlling parent, and operator to sign the agreement. Provide a way for affected residents and community groups to seek enforcement when a breach remains unresolved.

## Cost and trade-offs

In the worked example, $1.2 million in public cost triggers $180,000 in benefits. The developer contributes $100,000 for an existing library, $45,000 for workforce training, and $35,000 for transit access. The library adds 12 staffed computer-lab hours a week, including an evening, for three years. The city budgets $20,000 for monitoring and audit within the $1.2 million estimate. The city may share a public monitor with nearby jurisdictions to make oversight feasible.

## Delivery and proof

An independent trustee holds a letter of credit or bond sized to remaining obligations. The developer funds service in advance, and the independent monitor checks what residents actually received before public assistance is released. Before half the benefits are verified, at most 25% of the assistance may be paid. The government publishes results, holds unpaid assistance on a documented breach, and pursues recovery on an uncured breach.

## Next decisions

1. Pick the state and identify its local incentive, utility, and enforcement authorities.
1. Ask residents which services they would use. Have the library, workforce agency, and transit provider price them and define delivery records.
1. Ask the city finance director, utility, and legislative counsel to verify the full public cost, secure payment terms, and review the proposed resident enforcement route.
