# Data Center Community Benefits

Policy brief

## Decision

Choose a state and ask a legislative office to review a bill that sets conditions on discretionary city and county incentives for new or expanding data centers.

## Problem

A developer can offer a library new computers without committing money to keep the lab staffed. Residents need to see the cost of the public incentive and what the developer has agreed to fund before the vote.

## Recommendation

Give residents 30 days to review proposed terms for a discretionary cash grant or negotiated local tax rebate. Hold a hearing, respond to material comments, and sign an enforceable agreement that takes effect if the award is approved. The city or county selects the benefit based on local needs.

## Cost and trade-offs

In the worked example, a city weighing a rebate of up to $1 million negotiates $100,000 in developer funding for an existing library. That includes $10,000 for equipment and accessibility and $30,000 a year for three years of operation. The city budgets $16,500 separately to monitor the agreement. A statewide fixed benefit would simplify comparisons. Local selection lets residents weigh what they would use.

## Delivery and proof

The developer prepays $7,500 each quarter. The library adds 12 staffed computer-lab hours a week, including one evening. City finance checks the receipts and hours, publishes results, and can seek payment from the letter of credit after the correction and review process.

## Next decisions

1. Pick the state and confirm which local incentives the bill should cover.
1. Ask residents which service they would use and the library what it would cost.
1. Ask city finance and legislative counsel to test the payment, transfer, and enforcement terms before sponsor outreach.
