# School district AI purchases

Policy brief · Bill v1.0

## Decision

Ask a state sponsor to require public school districts to file a quarterly inventory of paid AI tools. Test the form with small and large districts before setting their reporting budget.

## Problem

A purchasing record can name the software suite without showing the AI features it includes, whether students or staff can access them, or whether any part of the price is for AI. Those differences matter before a school board renews a contract.

## Proposal

Districts report paid tools and bundled AI features to the state within 30 days after each quarter. Each entry identifies the tool, purpose, permitted users, whether it is enabled, and what the district paid. The state supplies a standard spreadsheet and the last return. Districts can certify an unchanged return or file a zero return if they have no covered purchases.

## Cost and privacy

The state provides the form, secure filing, and support. Districts use purchase orders, invoices, and access settings to update their entries. Measure setup time and quarterly staff hours in a pilot. Keep names, prompts, and student and personnel records out of the return.

## Worked example

A district pays $3,000 for a reading tool and $12,000 for a suite with a disabled AI writing feature. The reported identified AI spending is $3,000. The district lists the suite and marks its AI portion as unknown.
